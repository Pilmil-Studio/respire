Take back control of your phone without installing anything

Checked on 26 November 2026

Your phone can already limit the time spent in an app, silence notifications at night and make the screen less tempting. Here are those settings, in the order they help most. Allow ten minutes in all.

Names vary a little by brand and version. If you cannot find a menu, type the word in bold into the search bar of Settings.

1. See where your time goes

Android

Settings → Digital Wellbeing & parental controls. The dashboard shows time per app, unlocks and notifications.

iPhone

Settings → Screen Time. Turn it on if needed, then look at which apps come back most often.

Do not try to cut everything. Spot the one or two apps where you spend time without having decided to: those are the ones to rein in.

2. Put a limit on those apps

Android

In Digital Wellbeing, tap the app, then App timer, and pick a daily time. Once it runs out, the icon turns grey and the app pauses until the next day.

iPhone

Screen Time → App Limits → Add Limit. A message warns you five minutes before the end.

3. Switch the phone off at night

Android

Digital Wellbeing → Bedtime mode. It turns on Do Not Disturb at the hours you choose and can turn the screen grey. On some Samsung phones it is called Sleep mode.

iPhone

Screen Time → Downtime. Only calls and the apps you allow stay available, at the hours you set.

A good start: one hour before sleep, and the phone charging outside the bedroom if you can.

4. Pause distracting apps while you work

On Android, Digital Wellbeing → Focus mode: pick the apps that distract you, they pause while the mode is on, and you can schedule it, for instance on weekdays from 9 to 12. On iPhone, Focus does the same from Settings.

5. Make the screen less tempting

Bright icons and videos catch the eye. In greyscale, the phone becomes a tool rather than a shop window.

Android

Bedtime mode and Focus mode offer it. To keep it on all the time, search for greyscale or colour correction in Settings (often under Accessibility).

iPhone

Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Colour Filters → Greyscale.

6. Sort out your notifications

  1. Settings → Notifications, then the list of apps.
  2. Turn off anything not written by a person: suggestions, “trending”, streak reminders, what’s new.
  3. Keep messages from people close to you and what is truly urgent (bank, transport, work).

7. Three small habits

  • Move the app off your home screen. It stays installed, but you have to look for it: a few extra seconds, often enough to ask yourself whether you want to.
  • Log out after use. Typing your password again breaks the reflex.
  • Use the browser for social networks: the web version is often less comfortable, which is the point.

What these settings do not do

They help, but they have two limits. They can be bypassed in two taps, exactly when the urge is strongest. And they treat an app as a whole: you cannot keep Instagram messages while cutting only Reels.

Respire, to go further

Respire adds what is missing: a short breathing pause before opening the apps you choose, a wait that grows with each new attempt, and a filter that leaves the Reels and Shorts player while keeping messages available. A bit of friction before opening reduces how often people open an app (Grüning et al., PNAS, 2023). The app is free, with no account, no ads, and no Internet access.
